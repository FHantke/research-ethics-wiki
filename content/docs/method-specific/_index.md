# Method-Specific Ethics Guidance

This section provides ethics guidance tailored to different research methodologies commonly used in security and privacy research. Each research approach presents unique ethical considerations and challenges.

## Overview

Security and privacy research encompasses diverse methodological approaches, each with distinct ethical implications:

- **Human Subjects Research**: Studies involving direct interaction with people
- **Measurement Studies**: Passive measurement and analysis of systems or networks
- **Adversarial Research**: Security testing and attack research
- **Privacy Research**: Studies examining privacy implications and vulnerabilities
- **Cryptographic Research**: Development of cryptographic algorithms and protocols
- **Systems Research**: Design and evaluation of systems
- **Large-Scale Network Analysis**: Studying internet-wide phenomena
- **Vulnerability Disclosure**: Coordinated and responsible disclosure of vulnerabilities

## Key Principle: Context-Specific Ethics

Different research methodologies raise different ethical concerns:

- **Human subjects research** primarily concerns participant welfare and informed consent
- **Measurement studies** raise privacy and network disruption concerns
- **Adversarial research** involves dual-use and harm potential issues
- **Large-scale studies** raise questions about consent and scope of impact

## General Guidance for All Methods

### Identifying Harms

Regardless of methodology, researchers should carefully identify both:

**Direct Harms:**
- Harm occurring during the research process
- Impacts on research participants or subjects
- System disruption or damage during testing
- Privacy violations during data collection

**Indirect Harms:**
- Consequences from publishing research results
- Potential misuse of findings
- Broader societal implications
- Vulnerability to exploitation

### Ethical Analysis Framework

1. **Identify**: What could go wrong? Who could be harmed?
2. **Assess**: How likely is harm? How severe?
3. **Mitigate**: What safeguards reduce risk?
4. **Justify**: Why are the risks acceptable? What are the benefits?
5. **Document**: What limitations and caveats apply?

## Common Ethical Challenges Across Methods

### Challenge 1: Consent and Notification
- When is explicit consent required?
- What constitutes adequate notification?
- How much detail must be provided?
- Who cannot give valid consent?

### Challenge 2: Scope of Impact
- How many users/systems are affected?
- Is impact limited or widespread?
- Are there vulnerable populations?

### Challenge 3: Data Handling
- How is sensitive data protected?
- Who has access?
- How long is data retained?
- Can participants be re-identified?

### Challenge 4: Disclosure Timing
- When should results be published?
- Should vendors/affected parties be notified first?
- What is the responsible timeline?

### Challenge 5: Dual-Use Risk
- Could results enable harmful applications?
- Should sensitive details be omitted from publication?
- How is beneficial use balanced against misuse risk?

## Ethics Section Template

When writing the ethics section of your paper, consider addressing:

### 1. Research Methodology Description
Briefly describe what you did and who/what was involved

### 2. Potential Harms Identification
- Direct harms during research
- Indirect harms from publication
- Vulnerable populations affected

### 3. Risk Mitigation Strategies
- Safeguards implemented
- Protections for participants/systems
- Limitations on data use

### 4. Ethical Justification
- Why are risks acceptable?
- What are research benefits?
- Community impact

### 5. Limitations and Caveats
- What concerns remain?
- What follow-up work is needed?
- Any recommendations for future work?

## Coordination with Institutional Review Boards (IRBs)

### When IRB Review is Required
- Research involving human subjects
- Studies with identifiable personal data
- Experiments affecting humans
- Observational studies with privacy implications

### IRB Limitations
IRB review is necessary but **not sufficient** for comprehensive ethics assessment in S&P research because:
- IRBs focus on human subjects protection
- Many S&P studies don't involve human subjects but have other ethical dimensions
- IRBs may lack technical expertise in security/privacy implications
- Conference ethics review provides complementary assessment

### Recommendation
Engage with both IRBs and conference ethics reviewers for comprehensive ethical assessment

## Interdisciplinary Perspective

The research ethics evaluation should benefit from diverse expertise:

- **Technical Experts**: Understanding technical feasibility, limitations, safeguards
- **Philosophy/Ethics**: Ethical reasoning, principles, value analysis
- **Social Science**: Human impacts, vulnerable populations, community effects
- **Legal Experts**: Legal implications, compliance issues, liability
- **Domain Experts**: Method-specific considerations and norms

Research Ethics Committees should include representation from multiple disciplines to bring this perspective.

## Recommendations for Different Research Areas

### For All Researchers
- Start ethics reflection early in study design, not after completion
- Engage mentors and colleagues in ethical reasoning
- Consult relevant frameworks (Menlo Report, domain-specific guidelines)
- Document ethical considerations in your research record
- Be transparent about remaining ethical concerns

### For Junior Researchers
- Don't assume ethical practices from published examples
- Seek guidance from advisors and senior researchers
- Understand IRB processes if human subjects involved
- Learn from cases and examples in your research community
- Participate in ethics training and educational events

### For Program Committee Members
- Understand different methodologies and their ethical implications
- Recognize that some research has legitimate ethical concerns without being unethical
- Provide constructive feedback that helps authors strengthen ethical reasoning
- Refer papers to ethics committees when appropriate

### For Ethics Committee Members
- Develop expertise in diverse research methodologies
- Seek external consultation for unfamiliar methods
- Consider context and community norms when evaluating ethics
- Provide detailed feedback to help researchers improve
- Maintain transparency in your decision-making

## Continuous Learning

Research ethics is an evolving area. Recommendations:

- Review case studies and lessons learned from community
- Stay informed about emerging ethical challenges
- Engage with interdisciplinary ethics literature
- Participate in conference ethics discussions
- Contribute to community development of standards
